01 / The overview

Good stock.
A clearer shift.

BackBar brings bottles, stock levels, purchase costs and reorder decisions into one workspace for hospitality teams. A scannable overview connects the shelf with the next stock check or top-up.

Who It’s For

Bar managers and hospitality teams responsible for stock checks, purchasing and replenishment.

Design Scope

Web app UI, inventory information architecture, product cards, low-stock planning and an interactive product experience.

02 / The challenge

A clear problem.
A considered response.

Teams need to recognise a bottle, compare purchase and selling prices, understand availability and decide what to replenish. Those decisions become harder when product information, incoming orders and stock thresholds are separated.

03 / Design Details

The details that
shape the experience.

An overview built around the shift.

Summary figures, a shelf overview and low-stock alerts bring the key stock information into one place.

04 / The Design Thinking

Decisions with
a clear purpose.

How the information, interaction and visual decisions respond to the project’s needs.

Recognise before reading

Category names, subtype chips and bottle photography give teams several ways to find a product. The structure moves from category to subtype to bottle, matching familiar stock terminology.

Compare the essentials together

Quantity, location, purchase price and selling price share a product card. Clearly labelled rupee values and reorder thresholds keep operational context close to each bottle.

Account for incoming stock

The low-stock view considers on-hand quantities alongside expected deliveries. “Covered by orders” distinguishes products already being replenished from those that still need attention.

Keep a draft distinct from an order

Suggested top-ups support planning, while “Create draft” makes the intermediate step clear. Supporting copy explains that a draft does not send an order to a supplier.

05 / A Consistent Foundation

One experience.
Connected details.

An approachable typographic hierarchy supports a dense operational workspace. Blue actions, navy text and a quiet canvas establish priority; mint and coral provide supporting status signals.

What The Design Covers

  • Overview and stock summaries
  • Product categories and bottle details
  • Low-stock quantities and reorder thresholds
  • Expected deliveries and purchase drafts
  • Reporting context
06 / Design Outcome

A connected inventory overview.

A connected stock-management design that brings recognition, comparison and replenishment planning into one consistent workspace.

Stock overviewVisible replenishment needsConsistent dashboard navigation

Design outcomes describe the delivered experience. Live business performance has not been measured here.
