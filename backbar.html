01 / Project At A Glance

The product. The people.
Our contribution.

Industry
Hospitality & inventory management
Platform
Desktop web application
Primary Users
Bar managers and hospitality teams responsible for stock checks, purchasing and replenishment.
Core Need
Connect product recognition, stock comparison and replenishment context in one operational workspace.
Mavocado’s Contribution
SaaS product UX/UI design
Design Scope
Web app UI, inventory information architecture, product cards, low-stock planning and an interactive product experience.
02 / The Problem Worth Solving

When stock context is scattered, the next purchase is harder to plan.

Bar managers need to recognise products, compare quantities and understand what needs attention. A useful inventory view keeps those decisions close to the stock itself.

Product recognition

Recognise similar bottleswith clear visual stock cues.

Separated stock context

Keep quantity and pricingclose to each product.

Ambiguous replenishment

Separate planned top-upsfrom orders sent to suppliers.

Design ObjectiveConnect product recognition, stock comparison and replenishment context in one operational workspace.

03 / The Experience We Designed

One workspace. Several connected stock decisions.

01Move from the shelf to the next stock decision

The supplied workspace view combines summary information, recognisable product cards and low-stock context. It gives the team one starting point for reviewing the shift.

02See where the stock value sits.

The reporting view connects stock value, bottle counts and category comparisons in one overview.

03Identify the products that need attention.

Low-stock planning brings on-hand quantities, expected deliveries and existing drafts into the same comparison.

04Keep replenishment context close.

The supplied replenishment view keeps the remaining need and the draft action beside each product.

05Keep every bottle in view.

The inventory page combines recognisable bottles, stock details and category controls in a product-led overview.

04 / Decisions Behind The Design

The reasoning behind
the interface.

These are design intentions. The benefits below are not presented as measured outcomes.

01
Need

Find the right bottle

Design Decision
Use category names, subtype chips and bottle photography together.
Intended Benefit
Recognise the productbefore reading the details.
02
Need

Compare the operational essentials

Design Decision
Keep quantity, location, purchase price and selling price on the product card.
Intended Benefit
Compare key stock detailsin one familiar place.
03
Need

Understand incoming stock

Design Decision
Distinguish on-hand stock from quantities already covered by expected deliveries.
Intended Benefit
See outstanding needsbeside incoming stock.
04
Need

Keep planning distinct from purchasing

Design Decision
Label the intermediate action as Create Draft and explain what it does.
Intended Benefit
Keep stock planning distinctfrom sending a supplier order.
