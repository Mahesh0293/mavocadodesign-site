01 / The overview

A familiar task.
A fresh point of view.

Nothing Pay is an independent Android wallet concept inspired by Nothing’s visual language. It brings cards, everyday payments and spending visibility into a focused interface, supported by an interactive prototype.

Who It’s For

A design hypothesis: Nothing phone owners who make frequent mobile payments, use several cards and want a wallet that fits their device’s visual character.

Design Scope

23 screens and 35 UI states covering Home, Cards, Pay, Insights and Profile. Payment, bank-linking and security interactions are demonstrations.

02 / The challenge

A clear problem.
A considered response.

A wallet needs to make three things clear: which card is selected, whether a payment completed and how spending is organised. Familiar brand styling can create coherence, but it does not establish usability or trust by itself.

Nothing Pay / A closer look at the interface

04 / The Design Thinking

Decisions with
a clear purpose.

How the information, interaction and visual decisions respond to the project’s needs.

Keep the selected card identifiable

A prominent card, readable last digits and direct card controls reduce ambiguity in the design. Core payment actions remain close to the selected payment method.

Give payments a distinct finish

A clear confirmation includes the amount and a single next action. State feedback is designed to explain completion rather than relying on a decorative animation alone.

Put spending in context

Insights move from period and total to comparison and categories, followed by an optional breakdown. Today, weekly and monthly views provide different levels of detail.

Use motion to explain state

Card selection, verification and completion use motion for specific feedback. Production flows would still require authentication, decline handling, timeouts and recovery paths.

05 / A Consistent Foundation

One experience.
Connected details.

A dark canvas, restrained surfaces, red signals and dot-matrix details establish character. Conventional supporting typography keeps card information, amounts and transaction details readable.

What The Design Covers

  • Home and core money actions
  • Card switcher and card controls
  • Add-card and bank-linking demonstrations
  • Payment preparation and confirmation
  • Spending periods and category breakdowns
  • Profile, preferences and rewards
06 / Design Outcome

A connected payment design concept.

A connected wallet design study with a consistent visual language and explicit selection, progress and completion states.

Core payment flowsInteractive prototypeConnected app and web identity

Design outcomes describe the delivered experience. Live business performance has not been measured here.
